## Goal 6: 3D aerial maps

**Built.** Every service page in ResourceHub has a **2D map / 3D view**
switch (`servicehub-mvp/components/resources/detail/ServiceMap.tsx` and
`Map3D.tsx`).

- **2D** is the map the page always had (Google's embed when its key is
  set, otherwise OpenStreetMap).
- **3D** is free by default: MapLibre GL (5.24.0) with OpenFreeMap's
  Liberty style, which draws buildings in 3D from OpenStreetMap's heights.
  OpenFreeMap needs no key or account, sets no cookies, and is served
  through Cloudflare.
- The 3D view loads only when someone chooses it, has no fly-in when calm
  motion is on, and falls back to 2D with a short note if the browser can't
  draw it.

**Google's photorealistic 3D** (the note's preferred option) is one switch
away: `NEXT_PUBLIC_MAP_3D=google` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_GOOGLE_MAPS_JS_API_KEY`,
in the servicehub Vercel project.

- It needs a **Maps JavaScript API** key with billing on. The embed key
  already set is a different API and isn't enough.
- 3D Maps has been generally available since 30 October 2025.
- It is billed per map load (the Immersive Maps item): 5,000 free a month,
  then US$7 per 1,000. That is about US$35 a month at 10,000 3D views, and
  US$315 at 50,000.
- **Startup credits:** Google for Startups' Cloud credits don't pay for Maps.
  A separate Google Maps Platform credit (US$600 a month, separate
  application, unused credit doesn't roll over) does. You must already be in
  the Google for Startups Cloud Program to apply. Apply before turning Google
  3D on:
  https://console.cloud.google.com/google/maps-apis/credits/application/startups

**The other options in the note:**

- **"MapFinder" / "Path Finder":** no mapping product by either name turned
  up.
- **Google Maps Geospatial AR** (ARCore's Geospatial API): it works only in
  phone apps (Android, iOS, Unity, Unreal), not on a website. It fits a
  future Autinerary phone app ("point your phone at the building to see
  the entrance").
- **Google's Aerial View** (fly-over videos) works only for addresses in the
  United States.

Privacy: the privacy page lists OpenFreeMap, and Google for maps. The terms
now mention Google Maps' additional terms, which Google requires because the
embed is already in use.

## Goal 10: accessibility, documentation and debugging

**Built: "Listen to this page"**, at the foot of every page in both apps.

- It reads the page, or just the words someone selects, aloud. The app uses
  the voice, speed and volume from Settings, Accessibility; ResourceHub uses
  the browser's best voice at the chosen speed.
- Settings, Accessibility has a switch for it (on by default), and the
  choice and the speed reach ResourceHub through the hand-off (the tokens
  `listen-off`, `speak-slow` and `speak-fast`).
- Nothing speaks until someone presses it, and it stops when they leave the
  page.

**UserWay** (the note: "consider partnering with UserWay for the rest").
The recommendation is not to rely on it:

- An overlay adds a menu on top of a site. It can't fix the code underneath,
  which is what screen readers and keyboards actually use.
- Many disabled people and accessibility specialists ask sites not to use
  overlays (the Overlay Fact Sheet). Some say overlays get in the way of
  their own tools.
- It is a third-party script on every page, which the privacy page would
  have to list, and it costs money.
- Autinerary already has most of what overlays offer, built in and tested:
  - text size, contrast, dark mode, the dyslexia font, underlined links,
    calm motion, colour themes and colour strength;
  - spoken descriptions, voice navigation, and now Listen;
  - automated WCAG 2.1 AA checks of both apps, on phones and computers
    (`scripts/qa/check-accessibility.mjs`).

To try it anyway: set `NEXT_PUBLIC_USERWAY_ACCOUNT` (the account id from
UserWay) in **both** Vercel projects. The widget then loads in both apps,
and the app's privacy page lists UserWay by itself. Read UserWay's data
terms first. A better use of the money is a paid accessibility audit with
people who use screen readers.

### Resource Accounts (the Service Provider Dashboard)

**Built:** `app.autinerary.ca/business`, reached from **"Run this service?
Claim this listing"** on every service and product page in ResourceHub.

1. **Who you are:** a Service Provider or a Product Vendor, with your
   organisation, role and contact details.
2. **Claim your listing:**
   - a six-digit code is emailed to the address the listing publishes. Only
     a scrambled form of the code is kept; it lasts 30 minutes and allows
     five tries. At most three codes a day go to each person and listing,
     and three people a day can ask for one listing, so nobody can flood a
     service's inbox.
   - or the team checks: the person says how, and it goes to ResourceHub's
     admin **Requests**.

   Signing up doesn't confirm email addresses, so an account's own email
   proves nothing; the listing's email does. Each listing has one verified
   manager.
3. **Manage it:**
   - Changes that apply at once: the description, phone, email, website,
     free or paid, and in person or online.
   - Changes the team checks first: the name, picture and address.
   - Every change is kept with what it was before, and the team can undo
     it.
   - **How people use it**, as counts only, never who: saves, saves in the
     last 30 days, on someone's team, ratings and average, comments,
     recommendations, this month's Community's Choice nominations, and its
     rating standing.
   - "Is this the right picture?"
4. **Promotions** (the note's "promote ads"): a headline, a sentence, a
   picture, the searches to match, provinces and dates. They are drafted,
   sent to the team, and approved in **Requests**.

**Sponsored listings stay off** (`NEXT_PUBLIC_SPONSORED`): the privacy page
and the trust line say "we don't show ads". When the team decides
otherwise:

1. change the privacy page and `docs/voice.md`'s trust line;
2. tell people;
3. turn it on.

Then one promotion at most shows above results. It is labelled
"Sponsored", says why it is there, is chosen only by what was searched
(words, kind of place, province, never anything about the person), and
never changes the results.

### Ambassador Program (tentative, a proposal)

Ambassadors would be community members who bring Autinerary to their
school, town, workplace or group. Suggested shape:

- **What they do:**
  - share the petition and Community's Choice;
  - run a short session ("plan your next step");
  - collect what their group needs;
  - help a local service claim its listing.
- **What they get:**
  - free access (the "help advocate" kind);
  - a named page;
  - first look at new features;
  - a reference letter after a term;
  - for students, Uni Alliance club sponsorship.
- **How it would work:**
  - an application like Spot Guides';
  - a one-page guide written to `docs/voice.md`;
  - a monthly check-in;
  - no targets or quotas.

It needs the team to decide the benefits, and the lawyer to look at any
rewards (Competition Act, CASL for anything they send). Nothing is built
for it.

### The Positivity Quota

**Built:**

- A **note for today** at the foot of every page in both apps
  (`lib/positive.ts`, the same in both).
- 12 general notes, and notes for 21 norms. On some days the note is for one
  of the person's norms, and the norm notes for mental health and depression
  include 9-8-8.
- Written to `docs/voice.md`: plain, calm and true, with no "barrier" or
  deficit words and no urgency. A unit test checks these
  (`frontend/lib/positive.test.mjs`).
- Each place has the same note all day, so nothing changes while someone
  reads.
- On a service page, the sidebar shows a note for the person's norms, if
  they shared any.
- "Hide these notes" hides them. In the app, Settings, Accessibility brings
  them back, and ResourceHub follows the app's setting.

**Other ways to be uplifting** (suggested, not built):

- a gentle note when a step on the Path is finished, with no streaks;
- a private "things that went well" list in Reflection;
- people thanking a service ("this helped me"), shown to the service in its

- kinder empty pages ("Nothing here yet, and that's fine");
- error messages that say what to do next, without blame;
- noticing services whose ratings improved;
- a once-a-year look back, if someone asks for it;
- stories from Share your story on the home page;
- a thank-you to Spot Guides and Ambassadors in the newsletter.

## Goal 11, V1.5: asking services

**Built, not sent.** Two emails, each to the address a listing publishes,
once per service, with our mailing address and "reply 'stop'". Services that
reply stop go in `service_email_optouts` and are skipped.

- **The survey:** "What would (service) like in its ResourceHub account?"
  It shows the listing's picture ("Is this the right picture?") and links to
  the survey and to claiming the listing.
- **"(Service) was rated on ResourceHub":** sent once a month to services
  rated that month, with the same survey and picture.

The survey is at `app.autinerary.ca/providers/survey`, from a link signed
for one listing, so answers are known to come from that listing's inbox. It
needs no account, and takes at most three answers per listing.

**Sending is the team's call**, from `app.autinerary.ca/business/outreach`
(admins only). It shows who the next batch would reach, sends at most 200 at
a time, and only after typing SEND. It needs `RESEND_API_KEY` and
`NEWSLETTER_POSTAL_ADDRESS`. Under Canada's anti-spam law, an email to a
business's published address about its listing is generally allowed; the
lawyer pack asks to confirm.

**Turning answers into changes:**

1. After two or three weeks, read the counts and comments in ResourceHub's
   admin **Requests** ("What services want").
2. **Build what nobody disagrees with**: requests most services want that
   don't cost families anything (hours, booking links, languages, photos,
   accessibility descriptions).
3. **Put the conflicting ones to a vote.** These are requests that pull
   against families or each other: replying to comments (fairness of
   reviews), messages from families (privacy), promotions ("no ads"). Ask
   services in a second short survey and families in the newsletter (as
   Community's Choice does), then decide with both results.
